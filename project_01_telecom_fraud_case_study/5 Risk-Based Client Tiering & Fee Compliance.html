Context /  From static customer classification to historical compliance review

Regulatory requirement

Client classification affects the application of fee exemptions.

Regulatory requirements for small and micro enterprises made accurate enterprise classification relevant not only to customer management, but also to the legitimacy of applicable service charges.

Operational gap

Historical classifications did not always remain aligned with current information.

Customer attributes, annual-report information and classification records could change over time, while lower-risk fee transactions were not always connected to the latest customer classification information.

○ Project objective /  Reconstruct the decision at the time the transaction occurred

Customer
Who was the client? Corporate identity, ownership and operating information.
Classification
What type of enterprise? Reconstruct the applicable enterprise category at the relevant point in time.
Transaction
What was charged? Match historical non-interest income transactions and service fees.
Decision
Was the charge compliant? Compare historical customer status with the applicable classification rules.

Data reconstruction /  Building a reliable historical decision base

Customer information

Reconstruct the customer profile at the relevant time.

Reviewed industry, operating revenue, annual-report information and other classification-related attributes. Missing information was identified and supplemented through customer contact and internal records.

Historical transactions

Connect customer status with actual fee events.

Reviewed historical non-interest income transactions, identified relevant service-fee records and linked each transaction to the customer information available at the corresponding point in time.

○ Information recovery /  Filling the gaps before making a compliance judgement

01 — Identify

Inventory customer classification fields, historical fee records and missing annual-report information.

02 — Contact

Contact relevant customers and request missing information required to reconstruct their historical classification status.

03 — Cross-check

Match customer-provided information with internal business records and historical system data.

04 — Reconstruct

Establish a time-specific customer information set before evaluating whether the historical fee decision was compliant.

○ Classification output /  Turning a regulatory rule into an operational decision

Qualifying

Small / Micro

Historical classification meets the applicable regulatory criteria → review fee treatment → identify inappropriate charges where applicable.

Non-qualifying

Other enterprise

Historical classification does not meet the applicable criteria → fee treatment remains subject to normal charging rules.

Insufficient

Information gap

Missing evidence → customer contact / internal reconstruction → re-enter the decision process.

Remediation /  From identified inappropriate charges to customer refund

01 / Inventory
Full-period review Review customers and relevant fee transactions from the effective period of the applicable requirements.
02 / Validate
Evidence confirmation Confirm customer classification and historical transaction conditions.
03 / Approve
Refund list Consolidate confirmed inappropriate charges and complete internal approval.
04 / Refund
Customer remediation Refund eligible customers and retain customer acknowledgement records.
Existing customers

Refund through the original account where possible.

For active customers, approved refunds were returned through the relevant existing account and the remediation record was retained.

Closed accounts

Establish a traceable refund path.

For customers whose original accounts had been closed, customer confirmation and account information were obtained before completing the refund and retaining the relevant acknowledgement.

Process improvement /  Moving from historical correction to continuous classification

01 / Dynamic information

Keep customer classification current.

Establish a time-bound update mechanism for credit customers after annual or periodic reporting information becomes available, reducing the gap between customer status and the information maintained by the bank.

02 / System connection

Connect classification data with fee decisions.

Because lower-risk fee transactions did not always trigger customer classification checks, work with technical teams to improve data connections between customer classification systems and business platforms.

Project lens /  Why this matters for risk, data and decision systems

The project was not simply a historical fee-refund exercise. It connected regulatory requirements, customer information, historical transactions and operational decision rules, while using identified exceptions to improve the underlying process.

Process Design
Use historical exceptions to improve the mechanisms that prevent recurrence.

A compliance review becomes more valuable when the exception does not end with remediation, but feeds back into the system that produced the decision.
