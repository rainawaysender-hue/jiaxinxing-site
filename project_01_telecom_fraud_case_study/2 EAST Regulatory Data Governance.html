Context /  From fragmented identities to a common data foundation

Institutional context / 2015 — 2018

A unified enterprise identifier changed the regulatory data landscape.

Before the reform, enterprises were identified through separate registration records, including the business licence, organisation code certificate and tax registration certificate. From 1 October 2015, China began implementing the “three certificates in one, one licence one code” system, replacing the three certificates with a business licence carrying a unified social credit code. Existing records entered a transition period, with the unified code becoming mandatory from 2018.

Operational problem / Banking data

The policy created a common identifier, but legacy banking data did not become unified automatically.

Historical customer records had been accumulated under different information standards and across different business systems. Missing or inconsistent identifiers made it difficult to connect the same corporate customer horizontally across internal systems and to support complete, dynamic regulatory management of corporate accounts and historical business information.

○ Data inventory /  Classifying what was missing before deciding how to repair it

Customer-critical data
  • Customer name
  • Business licence / unified identifier
  • Legal representative
  • Beneficial owner
  • Payment contact
  • Online-banking administrator
Business-process data
  • Borrower / lender customer number
  • Business occurrence date
  • Business type
  • Other historical fields missing from individual systems
Governance decision
  • Customer confirmation
  • Internal record reconstruction
  • Cross-system matching
  • Regulatory-accepted completion where recovery was impossible

The first distinction was not simply “complete / incomplete”, but whether the missing field required customer confirmation or could be reconstructed from trusted internal records.

○ Dual-track completion /  External confirmation and internal reconstruction running in parallel

Track A / Customer & external information

Recover information that only the customer or external authority could validate.

  • Count and prioritise missing critical customer information.
  • Contact customers individually and set a completion deadline within the quarter.
  • Review dormant, long-term inactive and closed accounts that still contained missing information.
  • Apply for interbank data sharing through the regulator where appropriate.
  • For information that could not be recovered, use regulator-accepted placeholder completion so reporting systems could operate correctly.
Track B / Internal systems

Use existing internal records to reconstruct missing business information.

  • Apply for access to relevant internal systems.
  • Extract high-value identifiers and combine them into composite matching keys.
  • Match records across systems and supplement missing fields.
  • Identify recurring structural gaps rather than treating each missing record as an isolated case.
  • Submit system-optimisation requirements to head-office development teams.

Governance in practice /  Repairing data while improving the systems that produce it

Data layer

60,000+ existing customers

Critical customer information was inventoried, contacted, reconstructed and supplemented through a combination of customer confirmation, internal matching and permitted external information sources.

Business layer

200,000+ historical records

Historical business and information gaps were classified and reconstructed through related internal systems, reducing the need to treat every missing field as a new manual data-collection task.

The project was not simply a data-cleaning exercise. It connected regulatory requirements, customer identity, fragmented internal records and system design into a repeatable governance process.
