Context / From ad‑hoc remediation to a systemic recurrence‑control model

Problem / Audit recurrence risk

A centralized governance layer was missing.

Audit findings, recommendations and management letters were historically managed in silos, with limited coordination between business lines and compliance. The absence of a structured handover from “audit identification” to “business response” led to inconsistent remediation quality and repeated issues in wholesale banking operations.

Solution / A multi‑layer framework

Build a recurrence‑control engine, not just a task tracker.

The framework separates audit‑finding intake, historical risk screening, extended re‑check, root‑cause diagnosis, targeted remediation, and accountability. It transforms audit outputs into actionable risk signals, assigns clear ownership, and closes the loop through verification and model updates.

○ 01 / Finding intake & centralized governance / Who owns what in the intake layer

Audit / Compliance
  • Identify findings & recommendations
  • Issue audit letters & management comments
  • Provide initial risk assessment
Business line (response)
  • Verify facts and business context
  • Determine the operational root cause
  • Propose initial remediation plan
Corporate Finance (centralised)
  • Unified intake & tracking
  • Consolidate response quality
  • Flag for extended re‑check

The intake layer ensures every finding is formally logged, fact‑checked, and routed to the right owner before any remediation work begins.

○ 02 / Risk screening — 5‑year lookback / Turning historical data into a recurrence‑detection engine

Screening scope

All audit findings from the past 5 years.

Every audit finding, recommendation, and management letter is structured into a set of reusable risk dimensions: product, customer type, business object, system, rule logic, process, and control point.

Match rule

≥2 overlapping dimensions = trigger.

If a current business activity shares two or more dimensions with a historical finding, it is flagged for extended re‑check. This turns the screening process into a rule‑based risk model rather than a manual review.

○ 03 / Extended re‑check / Applying audit models to current business

Re‑check object
  • All corporate banking activities
  • Not limited to the original institution
  • Businesses with ≥2 overlapping dimensions
Re‑check method
  • Use original audit checklists
  • Perform self‑assessment
  • Document findings & gaps
Re‑check outcome
  • Substantiated findings → root‑cause
  • False positives → model feedback
  • New risk patterns → escalate

Remediation engine / Root‑cause diagnosis → responsible owner → corrective action

Root cause
Classification
  • Systemic — control gaps, rule logic, data linkage
  • Process — SOP, workflow, approval nodes
  • Execution — operational error, understanding gap
  • Conduct — integrity, conflict of interest
  • Model — control model miscalibration
Responsible owner
Primary accountability
  • IT + Business — system rule optimisation
  • Business Management — SOP / process redesign
  • Business Unit — training, supervision
  • Compliance + HR — conduct investigation
  • Risk + IT — model recalibration
Corrective action
Remediation measure
  • System fix — node enhancement, logic update
  • Process change — workflow, checklist update
  • Training / coaching — knowledge, execution
  • Disciplinary review — accountability decision
  • Model refinement — threshold, rule adjustment
Institutional diffusion

From individual finding to systemic exposure.

If an issue is identified in one branch, the framework assesses whether the same risk condition exists in other branches, business units, or the entire corporate banking portfolio.

Model feedback loop

False positives are not ignored.

When a re‑check does not confirm a finding, the control model is reviewed — turning the framework into a self‑improving governance system.

Accountability & verification / Five‑tier accountability ladder

Tier 1 · Foundation

Individual Responsibility

Validated findings are applied directly to the responsible individual's performance scorecard. No procedural buffer.

⬇
Tier 2 · Escalation

Recurrence (Recidivism)

Same finding ≥2 times within the same institution or account manager → penalty doubled, management accountability reviewed.

⬇
Tier 3 · Oversight

Management Responsibility

When issues recur, management accountability is assessed separately: did the control framework perform as designed?

⬇
Tier 4 · Materiality

Material Findings

Material findings disqualify both the individual and the institution from annual awards.

⬇
Tier 5 · Peak

Head‑Office Escalation

Head‑office‑level findings trigger downgrade in addition to compliance score deductions.
